Understand what the evidence actually supports.

Peptides are not one treatment category with one level of evidence. A medicine, a topical cosmetic, and an experimental injectable can share the word “peptide” while having very different uses and uncertainties. [4–6] (sources)

Read the labels

Prescription medicine
A specific product has an approved indication in a named jurisdiction. That approval does not extend to every formulation, dose, or advertised use of the molecule.
Cosmetic context
A topical ingredient is being discussed in a cosmetic setting. This is not approval to treat disease or evidence supporting injection.
Other clinical context
The history or setting needs explanation. Read the profile rather than assuming a current product is approved for your concern.
Experimental research
The wellness goal discussed is not established. A mechanism, animal result, small study, or testimonial cannot settle effectiveness and safety for you.

Quality and authorization

A certificate of analysis or third-party purity result does not by itself establish that a product is safe, effective, sterile, or authorized for sale. Product identity, manufacturing controls, and regulatory oversight matter. [5] (sources)

In Canada, injectable peptides are regulated as drugs. Health Canada warns against unauthorized peptide products; “research use only” wording does not remove regulatory requirements. Canadian and US rules are different. Do not read an FDA statement as authorization in Canada. [5, 6] (sources)

Compounding is not the same as a drug approval. Decisions about a particular compounded product require review of the relevant jurisdiction and circumstances. In Canada, compounding must not be used to bypass drug review requirements. [6] (sources)

Questions to bring to your clinician

  • What condition are we treating?
  • What evidence supports this exact product and use?
  • What are the risks, interactions, and alternatives?
  • What monitoring is needed?
  • How do pregnancy, breastfeeding, contraception, or other medicines affect the decision?
